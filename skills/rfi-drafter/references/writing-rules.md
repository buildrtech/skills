# Writing rules for RFIs

An RFI is answered by someone with less context than the person who wrote
it, usually in a batch, often on a phone. Write so it can be answered in one
pass without a follow-up call.

## Rules

1. **One question per RFI.** If the user has three issues, draft three
   RFIs. Bundled RFIs get partial answers and stay open for weeks. The one
   exception is a set of identical conditions (the same conflict at six
   grid lines), which is one question with a list of locations.
2. **Lead with the specific ask.** The first sentence of the question is the
   question: "Please confirm whether DR-2 may be relocated to ..." Context
   comes after. If the reader stops after one sentence, they should know
   what is being asked.
3. **Cite before you argue.** State what each document shows, with sheet,
   detail, note, or paragraph number, before saying they conflict. The
   reader should be able to open the cited location and see the issue
   without trusting the narrative.
4. **Quote short excerpts where wording matters.** A note that says "no
   openings permitted through beams without written approval" should be
   quoted, not paraphrased, because the answer turns on the words.
5. **Suggest only what the documents support.** A suggested resolution shows
   the design team the contractor has done the work and speeds the answer.
   A suggested resolution the documents do not support looks like an
   attempt to steer scope and slows it down. When in doubt, ask for
   direction and say what would allow a proposal.
6. **State impact honestly and briefly.** "Potential cost and schedule
   impact; steel fabrication release is waiting on this answer" is enough.
   Do not write "no impact" to make the RFI easier to answer, and do not
   quantify impact the user has not quantified.
7. **Name the waiting activity and its date.** A needed-by date without a
   reason is ignored. "Needed by Sep 18 so the fabricator can revise shop
   drawings before the Sep 25 release" is acted on.
8. **Stay neutral.** Describe the documents, not the people. "S-201 and
   M-401 show different conditions at grid C/4" rather than "the mechanical
   engineer failed to coordinate".
9. **Do not direct.** "Please advise" and "please confirm" are RFI verbs.
   "Revise the beam" and "the owner will need to accept" are not.
10. **Do not answer your own question in the subject.** "Beam must be
    relocated at C/4" is a conclusion. "Grid C/4: duct riser DR-2 and W24x76
    beam" is a subject.

## Common pitfalls

- Citing a sheet by memory that was not in front of you. If it was not
  provided, mark it "referenced but not reviewed".
- Asking "please clarify" with no specific question. The design team will
  ask what you want clarified.
- Burying the question under a page of history. Put history in an
  attachment if it matters.
- Setting the needed-by date to "ASAP" or to today. Give a date and the
  reason.
- Treating an RFI as notice of a claim. It is not; if the issue may become
  a change, the PM checks the contract's notice provisions separately.
- Proposing a resolution that changes a design intent the documents make
  clear (a rated assembly, a load path, a clearance) without saying so.
- Reusing an old RFI's text for a new location without rereading the new
  sheets.

## Weak versus strong phrasing

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "There is a conflict between the mechanical and structural drawings at grid C. Please clarify." | "Please confirm whether duct riser DR-2 may be relocated 2'-0" north of grid C at grid 4 to clear the W24x76 beam shown on S-201, or provide an alternate location." |
| "The drawings don't show how to get the duct through the beam." | "Section 2/M-401 shows DR-2 rising through the Level 2 floor centered on grid C at grid 4. S-201 shows a W24x76 continuous along grid C from grid 3 to 5 at that location, and S-201 General Note 7 states that no openings are permitted through beams without written approval of the Structural Engineer." |
| "This will have no cost impact." | "Potential cost impact for the shaft wall relocation on Levels 1 and 2 (architectural sheets not yet reviewed); no impact to steel if the riser moves rather than the beam." |
| "Please respond ASAP." | "Response needed by Sep 18, 2026 so the steel fabricator can incorporate any framed opening before the Sep 25 shop drawing release." |
| "The structural engineer should have coordinated this." | "S-201 and M-401 show different conditions at this location." |
| "Change the beam to a W24x84 with a web opening." | "If a web opening in the W24x76 is the preferred resolution, please provide reinforcement details; the contractor's preference is to relocate the riser so the beam is unchanged." |
